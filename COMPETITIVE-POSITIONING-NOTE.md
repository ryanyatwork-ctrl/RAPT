# RAPT Competitive Positioning Note

Prepared: 2026-08-31

Current market review shows that established vacation-rental pricing products do reference local events, so RAPT should not publicly claim "first dynamic-pricing product to use events" without a more exhaustive documented competitive review.

The stronger intended distinction is:

> RAPT is designed to discover and evaluate **future external travel drivers** before booking/search/occupancy behavior necessarily materializes, then use historical and current market behavior as calibration.

This is different from simply:

- detecting a demand spike after guests begin searching;
- inferring event demand from hotel/STR bookings and availability;
- displaying nearby event names next to market-based pricing;
- applying manually configured event/date overrides.

Public claims should focus on the architecture and user benefit rather than unsupported superlatives:

- `Price what is about to happen, not only what already happened.`
- `See upcoming travel drivers before booking pace tells the whole story.`
- `Forward event intelligence + property-specific relevance + explainable pricing.`
- `RAPT discovers why travelers may come, then learns how strongly that affects your property.`

Any future `first`, `only`, or equivalent claim requires a dated competitive evidence package covering relevant pricing vendors and event/demand-forecasting products.
