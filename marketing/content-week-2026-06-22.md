### Post 4 — Reddit r/AirBnB & r/airbnb_hosts (community-safe, value-first)
**Title:** How I stopped underpricing event weekends (event-based pricing, lessons learned)

**Body:**
Sharing something that actually moved the needle for me this year, in case it helps someone.

For two years my biggest leak wasn't empty nights — it was *cheap* nights. I'd be booked solid the weekend of a big concert downtown… at my normal rate, while every comparable place charged 40–60% more. I just didn't know the event was happening until guests mentioned it at check-in.

What changed:
1. I started checking local event calendars (Ticketmaster, the city events page, college sports schedules) at the start of each month and blocking out the dates that draw crowds.
2. For those dates I test a higher rate first, then walk it down if it's not booking ~2 weeks out. Pricing *down* from strong is way better than pricing *up* from cheap.
3. I track lead time. Event dates book earlier, so a slow week out doesn't mean lower the price — it means I priced right.

I'll be transparent: I do this with a tool called RAPT (I'm a user, not affiliated) that automates the event-watching and suggests the price, which is what got me to actually do it consistently. But the manual version above works too if you're disciplined. The principle matters more than the tool: **your rate should move when demand moves.**

Happy to answer questions on how I pick the multiplier or handle shoulder season.
